# Pelvis, hips and glutes

Deep reference for [../SKILL.md](../SKILL.md). The core distinction: **pelvic structure** (skeleton: width, tilt,
landmarks) versus the **soft-tissue hip curve** (gluteus medius, lateral fat, the flank pad). Femininity here comes from
a correct female pelvis read through soft tissue — never from simply widening the mesh.

## Pelvic structure

| Landmark | Read | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Iliac crest | The top rim of the pelvis; the waist ends on it | Registers at the side as the base of the flank pad; not a hard shelf |
| ASIS (anterior superior iliac spine) | The front points of the crest | Subtle bony points either side of the lower abdomen, softened by fat; the inguinal line runs from them |
| Pelvic shelf / bowl | The lower abdomen settles into it | Lower abdomen fullness sits above the pubic region and inguinal lines |
| PSIS / dimples | Posterior spines at the lower back | Two soft dimples marking the top corners of the sacral plane |
| Sacrum | The flat diamond between the dimples | A plane leading into the upper glute cleft, not a rounded bump |
| Greater trochanter | Top of the femur at the side of the hip | Sets hip width; softly padded; the thigh descends from it |

A female pelvis is relatively wider and shallower, with a forward tilt that, with the lumbar curve, sets the side-view
rhythm. Check tilt in side view: too little and the glutes flatten and the abdomen looks pushed forward; too much and the
figure reads as a posed pin-up arch.

## Soft-tissue hip curve

- **Gluteus medius**: the upper-lateral hip mass below the iliac crest; in front view it gives the upper hip its gentle
  convexity. A missing medius produces a straight waist-to-thigh line.
- **Lateral hip soft tissue**: fat over the medius and trochanter; the smooth outward curve from waist to upper thigh.
- **Flank pad**: soft tissue over the crest at the side, turning the front into the back.
- **Inguinal line**: from the ASIS toward the pubic region, the soft crease separating abdomen from thigh; it defines where
  the leg starts in front view and 3/4.
- **Pelvis-to-thigh flow**: in front view, the outer contour descends from the medius over the trochanter into the vastus
  lateralis without a bump or a notch; in side view, the front of the thigh starts below the inguinal crease.

Width test: if the hip reads wide but the skeleton landmarks (crest, trochanter) have not moved, the width is soft
tissue, which is fine within limits; if the entire hip ring was scaled outward, the body type has changed and the joints
no longer sit inside their masses (deformation and clothing will expose it).

## Glute anatomy

| Part | Description | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Sacral plane | Flat diamond above the cleft | Keep flat and readable; it anchors the glutes to the pelvis |
| Upper glute origin | Glute max originates along the sacrum and posterior crest | A gentle rise from the lower back, not a shelf |
| Gluteus medius (rear) | Upper-lateral quarter | Rounds the upper-outer contour in rear 3/4 |
| Gluteus maximus | Main mass, fibres run diagonally down and out toward the femur | Mass weighted low and medially, following fibre direction |
| Lateral glute | Where the max meets the trochanter region | A soft hollow can appear behind the trochanter in some poses; keep it subtle in neutral |
| Lower glute | Rests on the fold under gravity | Slight overhang medially, fading laterally |
| Gluteal fold | The crease under the lower glute | Strongest medially, fading out laterally, softer or absent in hip flexion |
| Glute-hamstring transition | Glute mass into the back of the thigh | A weighted soft transition, not a cut line; carries a lot of the realism |
| Fat distribution | Female-typical fat over the lower and lateral glute and upper thigh | Softens the fibre read entirely in neutral |

Desired: natural projection, believable weight, soft transitions, coherent attachment to the pelvis (sacral plane, crest
and medius all connect to the glute mass). In side view the glute projects behind the sacral plane and then descends into
the hamstring with a gentle convex-concave flow.

Reject:

| Defect | Read | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Two spheres | Round balls, no fibre direction, no fold logic | Weight the mass low and medially, add the fold and lateral fade |
| BBL-style projection | Extreme rear projection with a high shelf | Reduce shelf, return to sacral plane → soft rise |
| Extreme upper shelf | Horizontal ledge under the lower back | Re-establish the gentle origin slope |
| Hard lower fold | A uniform cut across the full width | Vary depth: strong medially, fading laterally |
| Fitness-model separation | Carved medius/max separation, hollow lateral glute in neutral | Soft-tissue layer back over the muscle |
| Flat pelvis + pasted-on buttocks | Glutes do not connect to the sacrum and crest | Fix the pelvis structure and sacral plane first |

Attractiveness here comes from coherent pelvis/glute anatomy and the side-view rhythm, **not maximum projection**.

## Pose behaviour

- Hip flexion / sprint: the glute stretches over the ischium and flattens, the fold opens and nearly disappears, the
  lateral hollow may appear; the trousers/shorts seat must follow.
- Deep squat: glute widens and flattens, the glute and hamstring compress against the calf; no candy-wrap at the hip.
- Hip extension / back extension: the glute bunches, the fold deepens.
- Stride: alternating fold depths; watch for the fold sliding onto the thigh.

## Review questions

1. Are the crest, ASIS, sacral plane and dimples readable through soft tissue?
2. Is the hip curve made by medius + lateral soft tissue, with the skeleton inside it?
3. Does the waist end on the crest with a flank pad?
4. Is the glute mass weighted low/medial with a varying fold?
5. Does the glute-ham transition carry weight?
6. Is the side-view rhythm (lumbar → sacrum → glute → hamstring) continuous and not arched for display?
7. Does it survive hip flexion, sprint and deep squat?
