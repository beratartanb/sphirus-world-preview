# Fitted-clothing readiness

Deep reference for [../SKILL.md](../SKILL.md). A good character body stays visually attractive when most of its anatomy
is covered. Do not sculpt anatomy solely for nude presentation: for most of gameplay the player sees the body through
clothing, at third-person distance, in motion. Garment construction, drape and cloth simulation are owned elsewhere (see
the routing table in SKILL.md); this file judges how the body reads through them and what the body must provide.

## High-value clothing-readability regions

| Region | What the garment shows | Body requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Shoulder line | The seam and the fabric's fall from the shoulder | A rounded deltoid cap and gentle trapezius slope; a square or collapsed shoulder is obvious in any shirt |
| Clavicle | Neckline shape, fabric tension at a crew/Henley neck | A real clavicle S-curve and a pit of the neck; lets a neckline sit and gape naturally |
| Breast / ribcage | A soft shelf with fabric falling from the lower-pole apex, lateral wrap in 3/4 | Correct lower-pole weighting and ribcage below; spherical breasts produce tents, flat chests no shelf |
| Waist | Fabric drawing in slightly below the ribcage, or falling straight from the shelf | Costal margin and flank pad; fitted-relaxed garments reveal the waist through side-view drape, not tightness |
| Pelvis / hip | Waistband seating, the hip curve under trousers/shorts | Iliac crest and medius; a waistband needs a crest to sit on |
| Glute silhouette | Seat shape, back pockets, fold line of trousers/shorts | Low/medial weighted mass and a soft fold; two spheres produce a doubled seat |
| Thigh line | Front crease, outer line of trousers/shorts, hem stance | Rectus femoris direction and offset outer/inner contours |
| Upper arm | Sleeve fall, short-sleeve hem stance | Deltoid insertion and back-of-arm mass; cylinders make sleeves look empty |

## Garment types

- **Fitted Henley / T-shirt**: shoulder line, clavicle at the placket/neckline, breast shelf and lateral wrap, waist
  suggestion in side and 3/4, soft folds from the axilla. Knit shows breast/ribcage honestly.
- **Jeans / fitted trousers**: waistband on the crest, hip curve, seat shape, front thigh crease, knee break. Denim hides
  soft-tissue detail and shows the skeleton and major masses.
- **Lounge shorts**: waistband, hip and seat through soft fabric; the bare thighs, knees and calves become primary reads:
  limb anatomy must be production quality.
- **Jacket**: shoulder line and silhouette only; the ribcage/waist read comes from the jacket's cut. The shoulder cap and
  neck support dominate.

## Homewear / fitted-relaxed direction

For outfits where body shape should stay visible without tight clothing (example: a soft fitted-relaxed Henley with lounge
shorts), the garment must allow:

- ribcage reading (fabric follows the ribcage side plane in 3/4)
- waist reading (a gentle draw-in or a straight fall from the chest shelf that reveals the waist in side view)
- hip/glute reading (soft fabric settles over the hip and seat under gravity)
- thigh reading (shorts hem and bare thigh contour; or soft trousers following the front of the thigh)

...without becoming skin-tight or sexualized: ease a few centimetres over the body, natural drape and folds, no
shrink-wrapped surfaces, no garment designed to display a single body part. The body underneath makes the clothes look
good by being coherent; the clothes should not be asked to correct the body.

## Body-side checks before and after any garment work

1. Neutral clearance and penetration against the target garments at key regions (shoulder, breast shelf, waist, hip,
   seat, thigh) — numbers are supporting evidence only.
2. The deformation set under the garments (shoulder elevation, squat, hip flexion, stride).
3. Silhouette in five views clothed vs unclothed: does the clothed figure still read feminine and coherent?
4. If a garment fits badly, fix the garment, not the accepted body. A body change for a garment is a body-type decision
   that needs explicit approval.
