# Breast and ribcage

Deep reference for [../SKILL.md](../SKILL.md). Evaluate breasts as **soft tissue attached to a ribcage**. Good breast
anatomy is never judged by size; it is judged by **attachment, shape, softness, gravity, transition and deformation**.

## The ribcage first

The breast sits on a curved, egg-shaped ribcage covered by the pectoralis major. The chest wall curves away laterally,
so each breast faces slightly outward (roughly 10-20 deg off the sagittal plane in top view), and the two masses are
separated by the flat sternal plane. If the ribcage under the breast is wrong (too flat, too narrow, cylindrical, or a
block), no breast shape will look attached. Check the ribcage in side and top views before the breast itself.

Ribcage landmarks that must register through the soft tissue: pit of the neck and manubrium, the sternal plane, the
costal margin arch below the breasts, the side plane (serratus region) between the breast and the latissimus.

## Breast anatomy vocabulary

| Part | Description | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Breast root (base) | The footprint on the chest wall, roughly from the 2nd to the 6th rib, sternum edge to about the mid-axillary line | Footprint wide and oval, not a small circle; follows the ribcage curve |
| Upper pole | Above the nipple line: a gentle slope from the chest wall | Straight to slightly concave or gently convex slope; no ball-like upper fullness in a natural standing pose |
| Lower pole | Below the nipple line: the fuller, rounded part carrying the weight | Holds most of the volume; rounds into the fold |
| Medial transition | Toward the sternum | Soft fade onto the sternal plane; a natural gap; no pushed-together cleavage in a neutral pose |
| Lateral transition | Toward the side of the ribcage | Rounds around the chest wall into the serratus/side plane; the most common failure point (abrupt edge in 3/4) |
| Axillary tail | Tissue extending toward the armpit | Fills the front of the axilla softly; connects breast and arm without a crease |
| Inframammary fold (IMF) | The crease where the lower pole meets the chest wall | Soft, curved, strongest centrally, fading medially and laterally; depth depends on volume and pose; never a uniform cut |
| Nipple-areola position | Typically near the most projected point of the lower-pole curve, slightly outward-facing | Consistent with gravity and the lateral orientation; not centred on a sphere |

## Correct target

Natural, soft, integrated, gravity-aware adult female anatomy: a teardrop-to-rounded profile in side view with the
volume weighted to the lower pole; a soft upper slope; a root that grows out of the chest wall; a lateral curve that
wraps around the ribcage; masses that face slightly outward; subtle natural asymmetry (one side marginally fuller or
lower, within what would go unnoticed).

## Reject

| Defect | Read | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Spherical implant | Round upper pole, ball on the chest | Shift volume to the lower pole, flatten the upper slope, soften the root |
| Floating mass | Breast looks placed onto the torso | Rebuild the root gradient into the chest wall; lateral wrap |
| Vacuum-packed geometry | Tight skin over hard volume, no softness | Rounder transitions, gravity settle, softer fold |
| Hard breast root | A visible ring/edge around the base | Blend the upper and medial root to near-imperceptible |
| Perfect mirror | Clinical symmetry | Subtle size/height asymmetry, soft-tissue only |
| Exaggerated cleavage | Masses pushed medially in a neutral pose | Restore the sternal plane and outward facing |
| Ignores ribcage curvature | Breasts face straight forward, flat base | Rotate orientation outward, curve the base with the ribs |
| Flattened / compressed chest | No volume, or the look of a compression garment | Restore lower-pole volume and fold before any size discussion |
| Pasted-on volume | Correct size, no integration | Work the transitions (root, lateral, axillary tail), not the volume |

## Pose and deformation behaviour

- Arms raised (150-180 deg): the pectoral lengthens, the breast lifts and flattens, the IMF shallows and rises, the
  axillary tail stretches into the arm. Check that the lateral transition does not tear or fold, and that the helper
  bones / correctives for the shoulder do not drag the breast root.
- Arms forward / crossed: the breasts compress medially and the lateral side stretches; no interpenetration at the sternum.
- Twist: the breast follows the ribcage, not the pelvis; watch for sliding across the chest wall.
- Forward bend: masses fall forward from the chest wall under gravity (a morph/corrective job, but the neutral root must
  allow it).
- Dynamic motion (sprint, jump): soft secondary motion is an animation/physics decision; the neutral sculpt must still be
  anatomically settled. Keep any jiggle restrained and physically plausible; never an exaggerated effect.

## Under clothing

Under a fitted knit (Henley, T-shirt) the fabric bridges from the lower-pole apex to the ribcage below, drapes from the
upper pole toward the shoulder, and shows the lateral wrap in 3/4. A good breast/ribcage relationship reads as a soft
shelf with fabric falling from it; a spherical breast produces a tent with a hard edge; a flat chest produces no shelf.
See [clothing readiness](clothing-readiness.md).

## Review questions

1. Is the ribcage under it right (egg shape, side plane, costal margin)?
2. Does the root grow out of the chest wall with no ring?
3. Is the volume weighted to the lower pole, with a soft upper slope?
4. Do the lateral transition and axillary tail wrap into the side and the arm?
5. Is the IMF soft and variable, not a cut?
6. Do the masses face slightly outward with a sternal plane between them?
7. Is there subtle, unnoticed asymmetry?
8. Does it survive arms-up, arms-forward and twist?
9. Does it read as a soft shelf under a fitted shirt?
