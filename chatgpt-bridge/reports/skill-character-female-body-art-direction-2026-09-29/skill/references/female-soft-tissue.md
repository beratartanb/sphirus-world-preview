# Female soft tissue and natural attractiveness

Deep reference for [../SKILL.md](../SKILL.md). Region specifics: [breast and ribcage](breast-ribcage.md),
[pelvis, hips, glutes](pelvis-hips-glutes.md), [limbs and joints](limbs-joints.md).

## Four layers

Reason about every region as **BONE → MUSCLE → FAT → SKIN**: separate, interacting layers.

| Layer | Contributes | Behaviour | Typical failure when missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bone | Landmarks, widths, hard planes (clavicle, sternum, costal margin, iliac crest, ASIS, sacrum, patella, malleoli, olecranon, ulna head) | Does not deform; shows where fat is thin | Mannequin / doll surface, "no skeleton under the skin" |
| Muscle | Directional masses, change with pose (deltoid, pectoral under the breast, obliques, glutes, quadriceps, hamstrings, calves, forearm groups) | Shortens and bulges, lengthens and flattens | Featureless cylinders, rubber limbs in motion |
| Fat | Softness, rounding, gravity, the feminine distribution (breasts, hips, lateral and lower glutes, thighs, lower abdomen, back of upper arm, flank) | Sags with gravity, compresses and spreads against contact, rolls in flexion | Carved fitness anatomy, vacuum-packed skin |
| Skin | Surface tension, creases at joints, folds where fat compresses, micro surface | Stretches over convex forms, folds in concavities | Rubber or plastic surface; noise if overdone |

The explicit rejection: **muscles carved directly under zero-body-fat skin** — separations, striations and hard edges
on a woman described as healthy and moderately active. A realistic moderately active woman has muscle *mass* and
*direction* readable through a continuous soft layer; separations appear only where the fat layer is naturally thin
(shin, knee, elbow, wrist, clavicle, sternum, lower leg tendons).

Female-typical fat distribution (general, varies by person): relatively more subcutaneous fat at the breasts, hips,
lateral and lower glutes, thighs (outer and inner), lower abdomen, back of the upper arm and lower back; this is what
softens muscle definition and produces the continuous curves. Do not strip it to "improve definition".

## Soft-tissue zones to check

| Zone | What realistic looks like | Common defect |
|---|---|---|
| Breast root | Mass emerges from the chest wall with a soft gradient; strongest at the upper-medial root it is almost imperceptible | Hard ring around the breast, floating mass |
| Underarm (axilla) | A soft hollow between pectoral and latissimus folds; the axillary tail of the breast fills its front edge | Flat plane or a crease that cuts the breast from the arm |
| Lower abdomen | Slight fullness below the navel, soft transition into the inguinal line | Flat plate or a sculpted "V" |
| Flank | A soft pad over the iliac crest, most visible in 3/4 and side, turning front into back | Straight waist-to-hip line, or a hard shelf |
| Inner thigh | Soft adductor-fat mass that meets the opposite thigh naturally near the top | Two separated cylinders or carved adductor lines |
| Glute fold | A soft crease where the lower glute rests on the upper hamstring, strongest medially, fading laterally | Hard cut line all the way across, or no fold at all |
| Back of upper arm | Soft mass behind the triceps that swings slightly with gravity | Cylindrical arm or carved triceps horseshoe |
| Knee | Soft padding above and at the sides of the patella; bone readable but cushioned | Skeletal knee or a featureless bend |
| Hip (lateral) | Soft tissue over gluteus medius and the trochanter, smooth into the thigh | "Saddlebag" bump or a hollow above the trochanter that is too deep |
| Lower back | Soft tissue over the iliac crest at the sides, dimples over the posterior iliac spines, sacral plane | Flat board or a hard lumbar crease |

## Gravity and weight

Soft tissue has weight. In standing neutral it settles downward: lower breast pole fuller than the upper, lower glute
resting on the fold, back-of-arm mass below the humerus line, lower abdomen slightly fuller. In other poses it redistributes:
arms raised lift and flatten the breast and stretch the axilla; forward bend lets the abdomen and breasts fall forward;
seated compresses and spreads the glutes and thighs. A body whose soft tissue ignores gravity reads as inflated or
vacuum-formed, however good its proportions.

## Compression and contact

Where two soft surfaces meet (upper arm on the side of the chest, thighs touching, calf on thigh in deep squat, abdomen
in forward flexion) the tissue flattens and spreads; it does not interpenetrate and it does not stay round. Where clothing
or straps press, the tissue shows a gentle, not deep, compression. These belong mostly to correctives and shading, but the
neutral sculpt must leave room for them (no maximum-projection neutral that has nowhere to compress).

## Natural attractiveness

Attractiveness is an outcome of the whole figure, judged by:

- proportional coherence (masses agree with the skeleton and with each other)
- body rhythm (continuous, offset curves; no straight tube segments)
- healthy mass distribution (neither depleted nor inflated)
- feminine soft-tissue flow (curves come from soft tissue over a feminine skeleton, not from widened geometry)
- posture (ribcage over pelvis, relaxed shoulders, natural lumbar curve; posture sells anatomy more than any sculpt)
- graceful transitions (junctions resolved softly)
- believable anatomical structure (the viewer senses a skeleton)

Never: a numeric beauty score; optimizing one part's size; directions like "bigger", "smaller", "hotter"; measuring
attractiveness by breast or glute projection. If the figure is coherent and rhythmic, it is attractive at a restrained
realistic level, which is what a grounded protagonist needs.
