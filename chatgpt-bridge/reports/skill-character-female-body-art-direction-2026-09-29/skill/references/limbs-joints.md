# Limbs and joints

Deep reference for [../SKILL.md](../SKILL.md). Limbs are directional muscle masses over readable bone, wrapped in a
soft layer. The three universal failures: featureless cylinders (under-sculpted), carved fitness anatomy (over-sculpted)
and wrong tapers (stick limbs or bodybuilder bulk).

## Thighs and inner thigh

- **Quadriceps** as one broad front mass; **rectus femoris** runs down the middle from the hip toward the patella and
  gives the front of the thigh its direction in side view.
- **Vastus lateralis**: long outer mass, highest on the outer contour; blends up into the hip soft tissue.
- **Vastus medialis**: the teardrop above and inside the knee; lower than the lateralis; in a woman it is soft, a gentle
  swelling, not a carved drop.
- **Hamstrings**: back of thigh, emerging from under the glute fold, splitting subtly toward the knee sides.
- **Adductors / inner thigh**: soft upper-inner mass; realistic thighs usually touch near the top in a neutral stance
  depending on the body; neither force a "thigh gap" nor merge the thighs into one form.
- **IT-band rhythm**: a very subtle long flat plane on the outer thigh between the lateralis and hamstrings, more visible
  in a lean body; keep it as a plane change, not a groove.
- **Glute-ham relationship**: see [pelvis, hips, glutes](pelvis-hips-glutes.md).
Offset rhythm: outer contour peaks high (upper lateral thigh), inner contour peaks lower (adductors + medialis near the knee).
Keep all muscle groups beneath believable soft tissue; avoid carved separations and striation.

## Knees

Structurally believable without becoming skeletal.
- **Patella**: a rounded, slightly flat shield, subtly readable; not a hard square.
- **Patellar tendon**: runs from the patella down to the tibial tuberosity; softly readable with the fat pads either side.
- **Condyles**: the femoral and tibial condyles set the width of the knee; the medial side is fuller than the lateral.
- **Parapatellar soft tissue**: fat pads beside the tendon and a soft pad above the patella (more on the inner side).
- **Popliteal region** (back of knee): a soft hollow framed by the hamstring tendons and the calf heads; in flexion a
  crease with compressed tissue, never an interpenetration.
- **Thigh-to-knee transition**: the vastus medialis teardrop leads into the inner knee; the lateral side is flatter.
- **Calf-to-knee transition**: the calf heads emerge below and behind the knee.
Deep flexion must preserve knee width and not collapse the front into a point (see [deformation](deformation-aware-sculpting.md)).

## Calves and ankles

- **Gastrocnemius**: medial head larger and lower than the lateral head (offset rhythm again); soft, not bunched.
- **Soleus**: the flatter mass below and beside the gastrocnemius, widening the lower calf before the Achilles.
- **Tibial ridge**: the shin bone is subcutaneous; a clean, slightly flat front plane; no fat here.
- **Achilles tendon**: a readable narrow band from the calf to the heel with soft hollows either side.
- **Malleoli**: inner ankle bone higher and more forward than the outer one; both readable.
- **Ankle taper**: a real taper from the calf to the ankle, not too abrupt.
Avoid bodybuilder calves (hard diamond), cylindrical lower legs (no calf heads, no tibia plane) and overly thin ankles.

## Arms

- **Deltoid** cap and its **insertion** about a third of the way down the humerus on the outside: a soft V where the
  deltoid meets the arm; gives the shoulder its roundness in front view.
- **Biceps**: a modest front mass; in a relaxed arm, barely a swelling.
- **Triceps**: the larger back mass; in a woman it is softened by the back-of-arm fat layer, which settles with gravity.
- **Inner upper arm**: soft, slightly fuller near the axilla, flattening toward the elbow.
- **Elbow**: olecranon readable at the back, medial and lateral epicondyles as subtle points; soft skin creases on the
  back in extension; in the inner elbow, a soft fold.
- **Forearm**: flexor mass (inner/front) and extensor mass (outer/back) offset along the length; the forearm is widest
  in its upper third and tapers to the wrist.
- **Ulna/radius rhythm**: the ulna ridge readable along the back of the forearm; the wrist is flatter than round (wider
  than it is deep) with the ulna head a subtle bump.
- **Wrist taper**: clear but not skeletal; the hand connects without a step.
Avoid stick arms (no deltoid, no triceps mass), bodybuilder arms (separated heads, veins, peaks) and featureless cylinders.

## Review questions

1. Does each limb show its direction (rectus femoris, deltoid insertion, forearm twist) through a soft layer?
2. Are the offset rhythms present (outer/inner thigh, calf heads, forearm masses)?
3. Are the subcutaneous bones readable (tibia, malleoli, patella, olecranon, ulna) without looking skeletal?
4. Are the tapers natural (thigh → knee, calf → ankle, forearm → wrist)?
5. Do knees and elbows keep width and structure in deep flexion?
